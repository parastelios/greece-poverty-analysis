The Greek Poverty Paradox

Two in three Greek households say they struggle to make ends meet — the highest share in the EU, every year since 2015. The official income-poverty rate ranks Greece a middling 7th of 27. This project investigates how much of that gap, 52.6 points on average over the decade, is real, and how much of it can be explained.

What the analysis finds, in one line. Reported hardship moves with specific material deprivations and a wide range of independently measured indicators — wages, purchasing power, accumulated unemployment and housing pressure — not just with the survey's own hardship question. Even so, the 52.6-point average gap remains largely unexplained: no single mechanism tested here accounts, robustly and on its own, for its size. All results are associational, drawn from country-level Eurostat aggregates rather than household microdata.
