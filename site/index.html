The Greek Poverty Paradox

Two in three Greek households say they struggle to make ends meet — the highest share in the EU, every year since 2011. The official income-poverty rate ranks Greece a fairly ordinary 4th of 27. This project investigates how much of that 47.6-point gap is real, and what explains it.

What the analysis finds, in one line. Part of the gap is arithmetic — a relative poverty line that fell along with collapsing incomes. Most of the rest is duration: not how deep the crisis was, but how long Greek households have carried it. Once accumulated labour-market exposure is in the model, Greece stops being a cross-country outlier. All results are associational, drawn from country-level Eurostat aggregates rather than household microdata.
